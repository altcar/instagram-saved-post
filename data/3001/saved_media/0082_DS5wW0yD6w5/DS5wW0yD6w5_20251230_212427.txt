Yes, sound can organize matter into geometric
patterns through resonance. The logo is based on a Chladni pattern, a well-documented physical phenomenon in acoustics and vibration science.

First described in the 18th century by physicist Ernst Chladni, these patterns emerge when a rigid surface—such as a metal plate—is vibrated at specific frequencies. Fine particles like sand or salt migrate to the nodal lines, areas of minimal displacement, making the vibrational modes of the surface visible.

Each frequency produces a unique geometric pattern, shaped by the material’s properties, boundary conditions, and the applied sound wave. Chladni patterns are widely used in physics, acoustics, and engineering to study resonance, wave behavior, and modal analysis.

For this experiment, I performed a controlled vocal test, singing directly into a Chladni setup. My voice acted as the excitation source, transferring acoustic energy into the plate. When the system reached resonance, the vibration stabilized into a diamond-shaped nodal pattern.

This outcome aligns with established physics: at resonance, energy organizes itself into stable, repeatable geometries rather than random motion.

That diamond-shaped pattern—generated by my own voice—became the logo of my brand.
It is not a metaphor, but a direct visual trace of acoustic resonance.
