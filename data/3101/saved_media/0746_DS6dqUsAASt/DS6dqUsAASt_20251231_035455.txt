Helvetica was designed in Switzerland in 1957 by Max Miedinger along with Eduard Hoffmann. It started out as a simple and neutral sans serif called Neue Haas Grotesk before the name was changed to Helvetica in 1960 to give it a more international presence. It became a key part of the Swiss Style and ended up influencing modern design in everything from major brand logos to public systems like the New York subway and has become the most popular font in the world.
