This time last year was my last day in the police. From as young as I can remember, I’d always wanted to be a detective. I dedicated my whole education to be able to achieve this, and I joined as a trainee detective constable a few years ago.

At first, it was everything I dreamed of; fast-paced, meaningful, intense. But eventually, that intensity began to consume me. I was attending scenes from nightmares, interviewing people who had done horrific things, and speaking to victims who were being failed by the system. All while trying to heal from past experiences in my personal life. 

I started EMDR therapy and was told I was showing symptoms of CPTSD. I was sitting across from people with the same trauma I was still carrying—being told I’d failed them. I realised I couldn’t keep giving from a place that was empty.

So I left. Despite the security, the pension, the expectations of my loved ones. For the very first time, I put myself first.

A year on, and I’m the happiest I’ve ever been. Eight months out of therapy. In a relationship that feels like magic. Working a job I love. And falling back in love with life again.

If you needed a sign—this is it. No job is worth losing yourself for 🫶🏼
