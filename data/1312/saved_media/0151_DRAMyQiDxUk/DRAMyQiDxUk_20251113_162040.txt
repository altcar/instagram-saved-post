If what’s wrong with me had a face, it would wear a tired smile — the kind that tries to hide a storm behind polite laughter. Its eyes would carry sleepless nights and unspoken thoughts, reflecting both exhaustion and resilience. It would walk quietly through crowded rooms, pretending to be fine, blending in just enough so no one asks too many questions. That face wouldn’t be scary or loud — it would be familiar, like the version of me I meet in the mirror when no one’s watching.

It would speak in whispers, not words — a constant hum of overthinking, self-doubt, and restless energy. Every expression on that face would change with the weight of expectation — sometimes anxious, sometimes numb, and sometimes lost in a thousand unfinished thoughts. It wouldn’t need to say anything; the heaviness beneath its silence would already be enough to fill the room. That face would be the reflection of everything I never knew how to explain — not broken, but bruised by too much pretending.

And yet, beneath the cracks and the tired eyes, there’d be something stubbornly alive — a quiet hope that refuses to die. The face of what’s wrong with me wouldn’t just show pain; it would also show endurance. It would be the face of someone who keeps showing up, who keeps trying even when it hurts. Because sometimes, the only thing stronger than what’s wrong with you… is the part of you that keeps fighting to make it right.
