SpaceX's Starship rocket is designed to take humans to Mars as part of the company's ambitious goal to make life multiplanetary. The Starship system consists of two stages: the Super Heavy booster, which provides the initial thrust to leave Earth's atmosphere, and the Starship spacecraft itself, which will carry crew and cargo to Mars. Both stages are fully reusable, meaning they can be launched, landed, and relaunched, which dramatically reduces the cost of space travel.

Once in orbit, Starship can be refueled by other Starship tankers, enabling it to travel long distances in space. This refueling capability is crucial for the journey to Mars, as it allows the spacecraft to carry more cargo and passengers by reducing the need to carry all the fuel from Earth. The spacecraft is designed to carry up to 100 people, with living quarters, common areas, and even entertainment spaces for long-duration missions.

On Mars, Starship will land using its powerful engines to slow its descent and touch down vertically. Once on the surface, the crew will rely on habitats and life support systems to survive, while Starship itself could be used as a temporary shelter. The spacecraft is designed to be capable of returning to Earth, refueling on Mars using resources found on the planet, such as water and carbon dioxide, which can be converted into methane and oxygen for fuel. This capability is key to enabling sustainable, round-trip missions to Mars.
