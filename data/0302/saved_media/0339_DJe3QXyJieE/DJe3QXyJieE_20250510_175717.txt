A hazardous TikTok trend dubbed the “Chromebook Challenge” is causing widespread concern across North American schools. Students are inserting metal objects like paper clips and pencil leads into the USB ports of school-issued Chromebooks, leading to short circuits, sparks, and even fires. This behavior has resulted in school evacuations, property damage, and, in some cases, criminal charges.

Incidents have been reported in various states, including California, Texas, Colorado, and Connecticut. In Colorado Springs, the fire department has documented at least 16 cases, prompting collaboration with schools to educate students on fire safety. Similarly, in Connecticut, schools have faced evacuations due to fires caused by this challenge, with one student hospitalized and facing juvenile court charges.

The financial implications are significant, with Chromebook repairs or replacements costing up to $400. Some school districts, such as those in Virginia and Oklahoma, are holding parents financially responsible for damages. In Oklahoma, families may be charged $274 for a damaged device.

Beyond the immediate dangers of fire and toxic fumes, authorities are concerned about the broader implications of such social media-driven behaviors. The trend mirrors previous hazardous challenges like the “Tide Pod” and “Benadryl” challenges, which have led to serious injuries and fatalities.

School officials and fire departments are urging parents to discuss the dangers of these online trends with their children. They emphasize the importance of digital responsibility and the potential legal consequences of participating in such activities. TikTok has stated that it proactively removes dangerous content, but the persistence of these trends highlights the challenges of moderating user-generated content.
