然而，這幅熟悉的城市風景即將改寫。發展局最新規定，公共建築工程需有一半採用金屬棚架，逐步淘汰傳統竹棚。這項以安全為考量的政策，也為四千名竹棚師傅帶來了轉型挑戰。或許，未來工地將不再響起竹枝綑紮的清脆聲，但這段香港獨有的建築文化，依然值得我們細細品味與記錄。

Walking through Hong Kong's streets, the ubiquitous bamboo scaffolding stands as one of the city's most distinctive sights. These temporary structures, ingeniously woven from bamboo poles, showcase the exceptional craftsmanship of local artisans and embody a unique architectural wisdom that values flexibility, resourcefulness, and efficiency.

However, this familiar urban landscape is about to change. The Development Bureau's new regulation requires half of all public construction projects to use metal scaffolding, gradually phasing out traditional bamboo structures. While this safety-oriented policy aims to protect workers, it presents a significant transition challenge for 4,000 bamboo scaffolding masters. Soon, construction sites may no longer echo with the distinctive sound of bamboo poles being bound together, but this unique piece of Hong Kong's architectural heritage will forever remain worthy of our appreciation.
