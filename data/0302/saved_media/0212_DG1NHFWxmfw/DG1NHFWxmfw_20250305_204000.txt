The Doll Test: Proof of Psychological Programming

Ever heard of The Doll Test? If not, it’s time to wake up. This test was created to analyze how segregation and societal programming impact children’s perception of race. In the 1940s, psychologists Kenneth and Mamie Clark designed a series of experiments where they showed children two dolls—one Black, one white—and asked which one was “good” or “bad,” “cute” or “ugly,” and “smart” or “dumb.”

Would it shock you that color didn’t just influence their answers, but it determined them entirely? Time and time again, children—even Black children—chose the white doll as the “good,” “cute,” and “smart” one, while the Black doll was labeled “bad,” “ugly,” and “dumb.” The conditioning runs so deep that even our own see themselves as less than.

The most disturbing part? This test has been repeated every 20 years since the 1940s, and the results HAVE NOT CHANGED. Even with so-called progress, the programming still runs deep.

Don’t believe it? Try it for yourself. Ask your child which doll they think is “prettier,” “nicer,” or “smarter.” See how much of the conditioning still lingers. And if you really want a reality check, try it on yourself. You might be shocked at your subconscious bias.

This is why representation, self-knowledge, and deprogramming are critical. If we don’t intentionally break the cycle, the system will keep shaping the minds of our future generations. The question is—what are you doing to reprogram yourself and the next generation?
