BREAKING: The UK Supreme Court has ruled that the legal definition of a woman is based on biological sex.⁠
⁠
Announcing the decision earlier today, Supreme Court judge Lord Hodge stated: “The unanimous decision of this court is that the terms woman and sex in the Equality Act 2010 refer to a biological woman and biological sex.”⁠
⁠
He added: “We counsel against reading this judgment as a triumph of one or more groups in our society at the expense of another — it is not.”⁠
⁠
The dispute centres on whether a trans woman with a Gender Recognition Certificate (GRC) should be treated the same as a cisgender woman under the UK’s Equality Act 2010. The outcome affects how trans women are treated in areas involving sex-based rights, such as single-sex spaces, services, and sports.⁠
⁠
In the 88-page ruling published in full following the judgment, Lord Hodge wrote that “the concept of sex is binary, a person is either a woman or a man.”⁠
⁠
The ruling states that trans people who hold a Gender Recognition Certificate (GRC) are still entitled to some sex-based protections under the Equality Act. ⁠
⁠
“The Equality Act 2010 gives transgender people protection not only through the protected characteristic of gender reassignment, but also against direct discrimination, indirect discrimination, and harassment in substance in their acquired gender,” it reads.⁠
⁠
The ruling has been met negatively by the UK’s trans community with some taking to social media to share their disappointment. Author Shon Faye said: “The British Government and the UK Supreme Court were never our friends … These people won’t stop here - they have a taste for it now.”⁠
⁠
Munroe Bergdorf added: Spending the next few days listening/speaking to legal experts about how this will impact the future of our community in the UK. In the meantime, please hold each other close. We’ll get through this together.”
